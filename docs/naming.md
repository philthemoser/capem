# Naming

## The decision

**CAPEM** — *Centros de Apoyo en Emergencias* (ES) / *Centros de Apoio em Emergências* (PT) / Emergency Support Centres (EN).

It is deliberately an institutional acronym rather than a brand. Humanitarian infrastructure is named this way — PAHO's SUMA (*Sistema de Manejo de Suministros Humanitarios*), LSS, DesInventar, Sphere — and the register matters to the audience. A tool a parish is being asked to trust with displaced people's records benefits from sounding like infrastructure rather than a startup.

The expansion also states the thesis: the unit is the **centre**, not a national system. That is the design's central claim about who the customer is.

A consumer-facing brand can sit on top later ("*X*, powered by CAPEM"). The infrastructure name does not need to do that job.

## What was rejected, and why

An earlier round considered short, evocative brand names. All were screened and dropped:

| Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Ayuda** (original working name) | Generic Spanish for "help". Unregistrable, undifferentiated. |
| **Baliza** (beacon, ES+PT) | Spain made the V16 emergency beacon a mandatory road-safety device category, creating both an SEO wall and a plausible trademark obstacle in the relevant class. In Brazilian Portuguese the first association is the parallel-parking manoeuvre. |
| **Cordada** (roped climbing team) | [Cordada](https://cordada.io/) is an IDB Invest-backed Chilean fintech operating in Chile, Peru and Mexico. |
| **Acopio** ("centro de acopio" = collection centre) | Purely descriptive, so weak as a trademark, and not used in Brazilian Portuguese, which says *ponto de coleta*. |
| **Escala** (aviation stopover) | [Escala](https://escala.com/) is a funded LatAm CRM; Escala 24x7 is an AWS partner. In BR Portuguese it also means a shift roster. |
| **Faro** (lighthouse) | FARO Technologies (NASDAQ: FARO), Faro Health, Grupo FARO. Worse: **in Portuguese *faro* means sense of smell** — lighthouse is *farol*. Breaks in the Brazil-first market. |
| **Enlace** (link) | Crowded across US-Latino nonprofits. **In Portuguese it primarily means a wedding**, so it reads as a bridal announcement in Brazil. |
| **Talón** (baggage-claim stub) | Talon Cyber Security (acquired by Palo Alto, $625M). In English *talon* is a predator's claw. In Mexican slang *talonear* carries sex-work connotations. |
| **Cotejo** (act of matching) | [Cotejo.info](https://cotejo.info/) is a Venezuelan fact-checking outlet in Meta's programme — an adjacent-sector nonprofit across the same region. |

The pattern worth noting: **Faro and Enlace both fail specifically in Portuguese**, which is easy to miss when screening in Spanish and fatal for a Brazil-inclusive project.

## Then the acronym space

Once the institutional convention was chosen, the obvious candidates were all occupied by Latin American government systems:

| Acronym | Occupied by |
|---|---|
| **SAPEM** | In Argentina, *Sociedad Anónima con Participación Estatal Mayoritaria* — a corporate legal form. Provincial utilities are all "X SAPEM". Reads as a company-type suffix across Spanish LatAm; search results also swamped by SAP. |
| **SIACE** | Argentina's AFIP tax-control system; Panama's MEDUCA education registry; a Venezuelan university system; a Ceuta pet registry. |
| **SICAE** | Portugal's official economic-activity classification system (`sicae.pt`) — fatal for the Brazil side. |
| **SICOE** | Mexico's *Sistema de los Comités de Ética*. |
| **SIRCE** | Mexico's STPS training-registration system. |

**CAPEM** returned no direct product, system or organisation collision.

## Remaining risk, stated honestly

- This is domain and search screening, **not a trademark search**. It does not substitute for an INPI (Brazil) or SIC (Colombia) filing search, which is where descriptive names fail regardless of what resolves in DNS.
- DNS resolution tells you a name has a record, not who owns it or whether it is purchasable.
- **CIAPEM** exists as a Mexican government IT association. Close enough to be worth noting; distinct enough that we do not consider it blocking.
- Acronyms are weak trademarks generally. That is an accepted cost of the institutional register, and part of why a consumer brand may eventually sit on top.

CAPEM is a **working name**. If you have a reason it fails in your country, that is worth an issue.
